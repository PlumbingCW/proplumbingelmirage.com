Your first ten minutes when water is already moving

Whatever the cause, the same short sequence limits damage while you wait for the plumber.

  1. Stop the water

    Close the fixture valve if there is one, or the main shutoff for the house. If you cannot find the main valve, say so when you call and the dispatcher can walk you through where it usually is.

  2. Protect people and power

    Keep everyone away from wet floors near outlets, and switch off the breaker for that area only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water.

  3. Contain what you can

    Use towels and buckets, lift rugs and move electronics and paperwork. Take photos for your insurance claim before you clean up.

  4. Call and describe what you see

    Tell the dispatcher where the water is coming from, what has stopped working and whether the main is off. That helps the plumber arrive with the right parts.

Why newer homes still have emergencies

Surprise is a growing city, and many of its homes are newer than those in older Valley towns. We do not state build years for any neighborhood because we have not verified them. What is generally true is that newer homes often have newer fixtures, yet they still get clogged drains, failing water heaters and leaking supply connectors.

Builder-grade parts are a common weak point in any newer house. Flexible supply lines under sinks, plastic toilet fill valves and original water heaters all wear out on their own schedules. When the plumber is at your home for one repair, ask them to look at those parts too, so one visit can head off the next call.

Older homes deserve a second look for a different reason. In the original townsite area, homes often have older fixtures, shutoff valves that no longer close fully and supply lines that have outlived their expected life. If a valve will not shut, tell the plumber right away. A valve that fails during a repair can turn a small job into an emergency.

What hardness means for your heater, fixtures and softener

Softer water generally means less mineral scale, but it does not mean none. Water heaters still collect sediment over the years, and aerators and showerheads still clog. If your water heater has stopped heating or rumbles when it runs, water heater repair often starts with flushing sediment and testing the elements.

If you are thinking about a softener or filter, ask the plumber to confirm your actual hardness first, since a neighbor's water may differ from yours. The city's 2026 local amendments list water softeners and filtration systems among residential permit exemptions. Confirm that with the city building division before you rely on it. If you want help choosing a system, softener and filtration service is the place to start.

Storm-driven power loss

Outages can shut down electric water heaters and any pumps in your home. If the power goes out, do not run water in a house where drains are already slow.

Slow drains after heavy rain

Heavy rain does not clog your drain, but a line that was already partly blocked can show it first. Call a plumber if several fixtures drain slowly at once.

Irrigation and hose bibs in summer heat

Plumbing code and permits in Surprise

The city published local amendments to the 2024 International Plumbing Code with an effective date of January 1, 2026. That document is the one a plumber working in Surprise should be following for new work. The city's own building division is the place to confirm what applies to your job.

For a repair or replacement, ask the plumber whether your job needs a permit and who pulls it. Do not assume either way. If a company tells you no permit is needed, ask them to show you where the city says so. Always confirm that the person doing the work holds an Arizona Registrar of Contractors license, which you can look up on the ROC website.

Before the plumber gets to your Surprise home

A few minutes of preparation before the truck arrives makes the visit shorter and the damage smaller.

Find and close the main valve

Turn it clockwise until it stops. If water keeps flowing after that, tell the dispatcher so the plumber brings the right parts.

Keep electricity away from water

Stay out of standing water near outlets, appliances and cords. Cut power at the breaker only if you can do it from a dry spot.

Clear the work area

Move furniture, rugs and valuables away from the leak or the drain. Open the cabinet under the sink so the plumber has room.

Call and describe what you see

Say where the water is, whether the main is off and which fixtures are affected. Photos on your phone help the plumber plan.
