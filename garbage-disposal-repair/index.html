Garbage Disposal Repair in El Mirage, AZ

Garbage disposal repair in El Mirage, AZ covers jams, humming motors, leaks under the sink and drains that back up through the disposal. If water is dripping from the unit or filling the cabinet, turn off the disposal switch and the water to the sink, then call the 24/7 line.

Most disposals stop for simple reasons. A hard object wedges the blades, a seal gives out, or a clog downstream pushes water back up into the sink. Some of these you can check yourself in a few minutes, and the list below shows how.

This page also covers when a disposal is worth fixing, when a replacement is the sensible choice, and when the real problem is the drain line, which is handled under drain cleaning.

When a disposal problem needs help tonight

Water pouring from the bottom of the unit, a cabinet floor that is soaked, or a sink that overflows with food waste are the main emergencies. A burning smell or smoke from the motor is also a reason to stop and cut the power.

A disposal that merely hums or does not turn on is not an emergency. Leave it off and call during the day, or use the 24/7 line if you prefer to get it sorted.

  • Switch off the disposal at the wall switch, then unplug it from under the sink if you can reach it safely.
  • Close the two shutoff valves under the sink, or the main if those do not hold.
  • Never put a hand into the disposal, even when it is off. Use tongs or pliers if you need to remove something.
  • Mop up water and keep the cabinet door open so it can dry.
  • Call the 24/7 line and say whether it is leaking, jammed or backing up.

Try these quick resets before you call

Many humming or dead disposals respond to a short routine. Keep the power off for the first step.

  1. Cut the power

    Turn off the wall switch and unplug the unit if there is a plug under the sink.

  2. Look for the hex socket

    On the underside, a small socket takes an Allen key. Turning it back and forth can free a wedged blade plate.

  3. Press the red reset button

    It sits on the bottom or side of the unit. A tripped overload will not run until it is pressed.

  4. Check the breaker

    A tripped breaker or GFCI outlet can look like a dead disposal.

  5. Restore power and test with water running

    If it hums and does not spin, or trips again at once, stop and call.

Fix it or swap it: a simple way to decide

Disposals do not last forever. A good rule is to repair when the cause is a jam or a loose connection, and replace when the housing leaks or the motor is failing.

Worth repairing

  • A jam that frees with the hex key and does not return.
  • A tripped reset that holds once pressed.
  • A loose drain connection or gasket.
  • A flange seal that needs resealing.

Time for a new one

  • Water leaks from the body of the unit.
  • The motor hums, then trips again after being freed.
  • Rust shows on the housing or the grinding parts are worn smooth.
  • The unit is loud, slow or jams often, and parts cost near the price of a new one.

The sink backs up and the disposal is not the cause

If water rises in the sink with the disposal running, the blockage is often in the trap or the drain line past it. A disposal grinds food, but it does not clear grease that has hardened in the pipe. The plumber can check whether the clog is in the unit or beyond it.

A kitchen drain that clogs again and again after snaking points to a longer buildup in the line. In that case a repair to the disposal alone will not solve the problem, and hydro jetting may come up as an option. Ask the plumber what they found and why before you agree to more work.

What should and should not go down the disposal

Small habits cut service calls. These are the ones that matter most.

  • Run cold water before, during and for a few seconds after grinding.
  • Cut large scraps into smaller pieces before feeding them in.
  • Keep bones, fruit pits, corn husks, potato peels and fibrous stalks out.
  • Do not pour grease, oil or coffee grounds down the drain.
  • Do not use the disposal as a trash can for glass, metal, plastic or utensils.
  • Do not use chemical drain cleaners in a sink with a disposal.

The dishwasher connection and the air gap

The dishwasher drains into the disposal, so a clogged disposal can leave water standing in the dishwasher. If an air gap is installed on the counter, water spitting from it also points to a clog in the drain. Mention the dishwasher when you call so the plumber checks both.

Hard water, scale and what it does to your disposal

The city's 2024 water quality report lists hardness from 4 to 9 grains per gallon with an average of 7. Over time, scale can build on the inside of drain pipes and the grinding parts, and that can add to jams and slow draining. A disposal cannot fix mineral scale, but regular cold water use and good habits help keep it moving.

If you have scale problems all over the house, a water softener is one way to reduce it. Ask the plumber if it applies to your home.

Installing a new disposal in a kitchen

If you decide to replace the unit, a few choices affect how long the new one lasts.

Motor size

A larger motor handles more, but costs more. A household that cooks every day may want more power.

Continuous or batch feed

Continuous feed runs with a switch. Batch feed runs only with the stopper in place.

Mount compatibility

The plumber matches the new unit to your sink flange and the drain layout.

Electrical connection

Many units hardwire or plug into an outlet under the sink. The plumber checks which you have.

Septic or city sewer

El Mirage is on city sewer, but check your own home if you are unsure.
