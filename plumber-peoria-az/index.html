Permits and code when plumbing work is done in Peoria

The City of Peoria permit page says a homeowner replacing a water heater needs a permit through the Development and Engineering Department. Minor plumbing permits are reviewed by online appointment, and the contractor must supply their Arizona ROC license and Peoria business license details. Ask your plumber who handles the permit and when, so there are no surprises at inspection.

The city code adopts the 2018 International Plumbing Code, with local amendments. Detached one- and two-family homes and townhouses are generally covered by the International Residential Code instead. We cannot tell you which rule governs your job, so ask Peoria Development and Engineering if you are unsure.

Gas water heaters and their venting fall under the International Fuel Gas Code. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first, then call a plumber. A leaking water pipe can wait a few minutes, a gas leak cannot.

What to do before the plumber gets to your Peoria home

These four steps limit damage while the dispatcher sends a licensed plumber. Do only what feels safe.

Close the main water valve

Turn it clockwise until it stops. It is usually where the supply enters the house or at the meter. If it will not move, do not force it, and tell the dispatcher.

Kill power near the water

If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, switch off the breaker if you can reach it without stepping in water. If you cannot, stay out and say so on the call.

Clear the area

Move rugs, electronics, boxes and anything valuable away from the wet spot. Take photos for your insurance claim before cleanup.
