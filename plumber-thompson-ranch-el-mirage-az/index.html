Plumbing in Thompson Ranch

This page is for homeowners in the Thompson Ranch area of El Mirage, Arizona who have a leak, a slow drain or a water heater that has stopped working. Call (623) 301-9042, describe what is happening and ask about plumbing service for your address.

Before you call, close the main water valve if water is running where it should not be, keep people away from standing water near outlets and take a few photos of any damage.

Where Thompson Ranch is, and what the record shows

The City of El Mirage uses the name itself. A public bid listing for a Thompson Ranch Neighborhood Roadway Mill and Overlay project, published in December 2024, says the work is part of the city capital improvement program for fiscal years 2025 to 2029. The listing gives no street list and no firm start date.

We found no published boundary for Thompson Ranch, so we do not draw one here. The firmest anchor is Thompson Ranch Elementary School at 11800 W Thompson Ranch Road, a preschool and kindergarten to grade 8 school in the Dysart Unified School District. Homes around that road are the clearest fit for this page.

If you are not sure whether your street counts as Thompson Ranch, it does not change anything for a plumbing call. Give your street address and describe the problem.

What homes from the 2000s need now

A house finished around the year 2000 is now more than 25 years old. That is well past the common 8 to 12 year life of a tank water heater, so the original tank may already have been replaced once, or may be due. Supply hoses under sinks, toilet fill valves and faucet cartridges have also had time to wear out.

This is general plumbing information, not a claim about any particular house on your street. We have not inspected the homes here and we do not know what pipe material any one of them uses.

Water is a second reason to check things. The City of El Mirage 2024 water quality report lists hardness between 4 and 9 grains per gallon, averaging 7. That figure is citywide, so we cannot say what your tap reads. Hard water leaves scale on heating elements and faucet aerators over the years.

Signs it is time to call

None of these is proof of a particular cause, but each one is a good reason to have a plumber look.

  • A water heater that makes popping or rumbling noises, or leaves a wet ring on the floor.
  • Hot water that runs out much sooner than it used to.
  • A toilet that keeps running or refills on its own.
  • A water meter that moves when every fixture is off.
  • Water stains on a ceiling or on a wall shared with a bathroom.
  • A drain that clears and then slows again within days.

The street resurfacing project and your pipes

The Thompson Ranch roadway project is described as removing the top 1.5 to 2 inches of pavement and laying new asphalt on selected neighborhood roads. That is surface work. It is not a water or sewer project, and we found nothing tying it to plumbing problems at the homes along those streets.

If anyone, including a homeowner, plans to dig in a yard or near the street, Arizona 811 has to be called first so buried lines can be marked. Ask about it before any trenching starts.

Before you call about a Thompson Ranch home

A few minutes of preparation makes the call shorter and the visit more useful.

Find the main shutoff

It is usually at the front of the house or at the meter. Close it if water is spraying or pooling.

Handle the water heater

If the tank is leaking, close its cold-water valve and turn the thermostat down. Do not touch wet electrical parts.

Take photos

Photograph stains, puddles and the label on the water heater. Note when the problem began.

Clear the way

Move items away from the sink, tank or utility closet so a plumber can reach the problem quickly.
