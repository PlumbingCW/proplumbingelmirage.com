Plumbing in Original Town Site

The Original Town Site is the part of El Mirage, Arizona that the city calls the original square mile. This page is for people with homes or small buildings there who have a leak, a clogged drain or a water heater problem. Call (623) 301-9042, describe what is happening and ask about plumbing service.

If water is spraying or pooling, close the main valve first. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 instead of a plumber.

The first square mile of El Mirage

Settlers, many of them farm workers, built homes near Grand Avenue on the west bank of the Agua Fria River in the early 1930s. El Mirage was incorporated on June 25, 1951, and it is celebrating its 75th anniversary in 2026.

A November 2025 city resolution calls the area the Original Town Site and describes it as the original square mile of El Mirage. We found no street list for it, so we do not draw its boundary here. If you are unsure whether your address is inside it, ask the city.

What the city says it plans to do here

The city applied for fiscal year 2026 to 2027 Community Development Block Grant money for El Mirage Original Town Site Roadway Improvements. The request is for $550,000 and covers resurfacing streets, stormwater upgrades and ADA sidewalk improvements in the original square mile.

This is an application, and we did not find confirmation that it was awarded. Work like this can involve digging near streets and curbs, so a homeowner who plans any excavation should call Arizona 811 first so buried lines are marked.

Old streets do not mean old pipes

Most El Mirage homes were built much later. The median build year is about 2004 and roughly 63 percent of the city housing was built from 2000 to 2009. So a house on an old street can be new, and the reverse can also be true.

The honest approach is to look at the house itself. Check the age of the water heater, the shutoff valves and the visible supply lines, rather than assuming a material from the street.

Things worth checking in an older house

This list is general. It applies to any home that has gone a long time without plumbing work.

  • Test the main shutoff and each fixture shutoff, gently, while nothing is wrong.
  • Look for green or white crust at joints and under sinks.
  • Read the water heater label for its age and note any rust at the base.
  • Watch the meter with every fixture off for signs of a hidden leak.
  • Note any slow drains and which fixtures they involve.

Monsoon runoff and drainage

Monsoon season runs from June 15 to September 30, and the planned stormwater work shows the city sees drainage as a concern in this area. Heavy rain can send water toward yards and low drains.

Keep cleanouts and drain covers clear before the season, and photograph any water that enters a building so you have a record. A plumber can help with a drain inside the building, while street drainage is a city matter.
