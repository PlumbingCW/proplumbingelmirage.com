Drain Cleaning in El Mirage, AZ

Drain cleaning in El Mirage, AZ means removing whatever is blocking a drain pipe and then working out why it blocked. A drain is a gravity pipe, so grease, hair, wipes, or roots narrow it until water stops moving. Our 24/7 emergency line is answered at any hour, and if water is rising right now, stop using every fixture in the house while you wait.

Most clogs start small. Water drains a little slower for weeks, then one heavy load of laundry or a holiday dinner finishes the job. If the same drain has been snaked before and clogged again, the real fix is usually a different tool or a look inside the pipe, which is covered below.

This page covers kitchen and bathroom clogs, how snaking works and where it stops, and what to do about yard drains. If sewage is coming up in a tub or floor drain, read about clearing the main sewer line as well. Neighbors in Youngtown can read about plumbers serving Youngtown next door.

When a clogged drain is an emergency

A single slow sink can wait for a daytime visit. Sewage in a tub, shower, or floor drain cannot, and neither can a toilet that overflows when you run the washing machine. Those signs usually point to a blockage in the main line, not the fixture.

Wastewater carries bacteria, so keep children and pets out of any room where it has surfaced.

  • Stop running water. No flushing, no laundry, no dishwasher.
  • Keep people and pets away from any backed-up floor area.
  • Turn off power to wet outlets only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water.
  • Do not pour drain chemicals into a drain that is already full of water.
  • Call the 24/7 line and say which fixtures are backing up.

Kitchen sink clogs and what causes them

Kitchen drains clog differently from bathroom drains. Cooking grease is liquid when it goes down the pipe and hardens as it cools, coating the walls a little more with each meal. Coffee grounds, rice, and starchy food scraps then stick to that coating and build a plug.

A garbage disposal grinds food finely but does not remove it from the pipe. If the clog sits just past the disposal, the plumber may need to check the disposal itself before cabling the line. Our garbage disposal repair page covers jams and humming units.

The fastest way to prevent a repeat is simple. Scrape plates into the trash, wipe pans with a paper towel before washing, and run hot water for a short while after the sink is used. None of that clears an existing clog, but it keeps a freshly cleared line open longer.

Try this before you call, and what to skip

Some clogs clear with a few minutes of effort. Others get worse if you push at them the wrong way.

  • A plunger works on a single sink or tub if you seal the overflow opening with a wet cloth first.
  • Remove and clean the sink stopper. Hair often wraps around it and does most of the blocking.
  • Place a bucket under the trap and unscrew it by hand to check for a plug of debris.
  • Skip liquid drain openers when water is standing in the fixture. They can splash and burn skin and can damage some pipes if they sit.
  • Never mix two cleaning products in a drain. Some combinations release harmful fumes.
  • Stop if two or more fixtures are affected. That points past the fixture to a shared line.

How snaking and cabling work, and where they fall short

Snaking, also called cabling, feeds a rotating steel cable into the pipe until it reaches the blockage. The cable head either breaks the clog apart or hooks it so it can be pulled out. For hair, soap scum, and ordinary kitchen buildup, this is quick and works well.

The method has limits. A cable bores a hole through a clog and leaves the pipe walls coated, so grease and scale can start building again. A cable also cannot tell you whether the pipe is cracked, sagging, or invaded by roots.

That is why a plumber may suggest a camera look or hydro jetting when a line keeps clogging. Jetting scours the pipe walls with high-pressure water and handles buildup that a cable only pokes through. It is not right for every pipe, and the plumber should explain why before using it.

Snake or step up? How the options compare

Neither method is better in every case. The choice depends on what is in the pipe and how often it has clogged.

Cable or snake is usually enough when

  • This is the first clog in that line in a long time.
  • The blockage is hair, soap, or a small kitchen plug.
  • Only one fixture is affected.
  • The cable comes back with a clear sign of the cause, such as a wad of hair or wipes.

Consider a camera or jetting when

  • The line has been snaked and clogged again within weeks or months.
  • The cable comes back with roots or scale and the water slows again soon after.
  • Several fixtures back up together.
  • You want to know whether a pipe is cracked or sagging before spending more on clearing it.

How a drain cleaning visit usually goes

The exact steps depend on the clog, but most visits follow this order.

  1. Ask what changed

    The plumber asks which fixtures are slow, when it started, and what has been tried. This narrows down where the clog sits.

  2. Test the fixtures

    Running water in several fixtures shows whether the blockage is local or in a shared line.

  3. Find a cleanout or access point

    Cabling from a cleanout is cleaner than pulling a toilet. The plumber picks the access that reaches the clog.

  4. Clear the line

    The cable goes in until the plumber feels the blockage give, then the line is flushed with water to confirm it runs freely.

  5. Explain what came out

    What the cable brought back often explains the cause. Grease, roots, and hair each point to a different prevention step.

  6. Quote any further work

    If the line needs jetting, a camera look, or a repair, you get a clear price before work starts.

Snaked it and it clogged again

A repeat clog usually means the first cleaning removed the symptom and not the cause. Grease coating, mineral scale, tree roots, a sagging section of pipe, or a belly where water pools can each bring a clog back.

The city's 2024 water quality report lists hardness from 4 to 9 grains per gallon, with an average of 7. Hard water can leave scale on fixtures and react with soap to form scum, which gives clogs something to grab. Hardness is rarely the only cause, so do not blame it before the plumber has looked.

Ask the plumber what came out on the cable and whether a camera look makes sense. A line that clogs three times in a year is cheaper to inspect once than to clear again and again. Our sewer inspection page explains what a camera can and cannot show.

Yard drains, storm drains, and slow bathroom sinks

Yard drains and storm drains are a different job from indoor drains. A yard drain on private property collects runoff and often fills with leaves, soil, and gravel. A plumber can clear one with a cable or water, but the cause is usually debris washing in, so the fix is cleaning it out, not a pipe repair.

Street storm drains and public drainage belong to the city, not to you. If water pools in the street after rain, ask the city. Monsoon season runs June 15 to September 30, so check any yard drain before the storms start.

A slow bathroom sink is most often hair and soap on the stopper or in the trap. Pull the stopper, clean it, and try the trap before calling. If both are clean and it is still slow, the branch line behind the wall is the next place to look.

A wet spot near a drain line is not a clog

Sometimes a drain problem is a leak and not a blockage. A damp patch on a wall or ceiling near a drain, a musty smell, or a floor that feels soft around a tub can mean a drain line is leaking.

Clearing a clog will not stop a leak. If you see moisture and the drain also runs slowly, tell the plumber so the leak gets checked. Our leak detection page covers how hidden leaks are located.
