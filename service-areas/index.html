What to have ready when you call

Give the dispatcher your street address and ZIP code first, because that confirms the town and which page applies to you. Then describe what you see, hear or smell and when it started. Say whether you already shut off the main valve.

Mention gate codes, community access rules and pets. Many homes in newer subdivisions sit behind gates, and a plumber who cannot get in cannot start. If you will not be home, tell the dispatcher who can meet the plumber.

If the problem involves a gas smell, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first. Then call the plumbing line once you are safe.

How dispatch decides where to go

The 24/7 line starts with your address. Every emergency call is judged by what is happening, such as water that will not stop or sewage in the house, and by how soon a licensed plumber can be lined up for that address.
