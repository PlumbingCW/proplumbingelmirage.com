What a Sewer Camera Inspection Shows in El Mirage, AZ

A small camera on a flexible line lets a plumber look inside your drain pipe without digging.

The short answer

A sewer camera inspection shows the inside of your main drain line. The plumber feeds a waterproof camera through a cleanout and watches a live video of the pipe. It can reveal roots, cracks, broken or offset joints, sags that hold water, grease buildup, and blockages, along with an estimate of where each problem sits.

Get one when drains back up more than once, before you buy a house, before you spend money on a repair, or after cleaning that did not last. Ask first whether your home is on a city sewer or a septic system, because the inspection and the follow-up differ.

Is your home on city sewer or septic?

Do not assume. Ask before you book, because the two systems need different inspections.

City sewer connection

  • The main line runs from the house to a connection in the street or an easement.
  • The camera usually enters through a cleanout near the house.
  • The utility may own part of the line, so ask where your responsibility ends.
  • Repairs on the street side may involve the city.
  • Excavation must be marked through Arizona 811 first.

Septic system

  • Waste goes to a tank and drain field on the property.
  • A camera can look at the line to the tank, but the tank and field need their own inspection.
  • Pumping history and tank location matter.
  • A clog may be in the tank, baffle or field and not the pipe.
  • Ask the plumber what a camera can and cannot assess for your system.

When is a camera worth the visit?

Repeat backups

A drain that clogs again after cleaning may have a root, a belly or a crack that cleaning alone will not fix.

Slow drains in several fixtures

If a tub, a shower and a floor drain all slow at once, the problem is likely in the main line and not in one fixture trap.

Buying or selling a home

A recorded inspection of the sewer line before closing can turn up repairs that a standard home inspection does not cover.

Before a big repair

Video confirms where the damage is, so the repair covers the right section and not a guess.

Gurgling or sewer smell

Odors or noises can come from a venting problem, a dry trap or a partial blockage. The camera helps tell which.

After a repair

A second look shows whether the new section is aligned and the line is clear.

What happens during the inspection, step by step

  1. Find the access point

    The plumber looks for a cleanout, usually near the house, or pulls a toilet if none exists.

  2. Clear the way if needed

    A badly blocked pipe may need drain cleaning first, so the camera can pass.

  3. Feed the camera

    A flexible cable carries a small camera and light down the pipe while a screen shows the view.

  4. Watch and mark

    A locator in the camera head lets the plumber trace its position above ground, so a problem can be placed on the lot.

  5. Record the video

    Ask for a copy of the footage and for the problem points to be described in writing.

  6. Review the options

    The plumber explains what was seen, what needs action, and what can wait.

What to ask before you book

  • Will I get the video and a written summary?
  • Does the price include locating the problem on the lot?
  • What happens if the camera cannot get past a blockage?
  • Is the line on city sewer, and who owns which part?
  • What do you recommend if you find roots, and what are the trade-offs?
  • Can you describe repair options, not only the one you prefer?
  • Is the price confirmed before work starts?

After the camera: the repair choices

Findings fall into groups. Light roots or buildup may respond to cleaning with a cutter or high-pressure water, and hydro-jetting is one method plumbers use for that. Cracks, breaks and bellies usually need a repair or replacement of that section.

For those, ask about sewer line repair options such as spot digging, relining and full replacement. Each has trade-offs in cost, disruption and how long it should last. A plumber can explain which fit your pipe and your yard.

Do not authorize a full replacement from one glance at the video. Ask to see the problem on screen and ask what happens if you wait.

Honest limits of a camera

A camera shows the inside of the pipe on the day it runs. It will not predict the next root intrusion, and it cannot see through standing water or past a blockage.

A clean video is good news but not a promise. If symptoms return, the line may need a second look after cleaning or after a rainy stretch.

Census-based data puts the median El Mirage home at about 2004, and most homes date from the 2000s boom. That suggests newer lines for many homes, but it does not tell you your pipe material, whether it has settled, or what connection you have. A camera answers that for your lot.

Before any digging for a repair, Arizona 811 requires a call, the service is free, and utilities have two full working days to respond. Ask the plumber who requests the marking and how that fits the schedule. If the repair needs a permit, ask the El Mirage building department.
