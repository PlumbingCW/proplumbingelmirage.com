Sewer Inspection in El Mirage, AZ

A sewer inspection in El Mirage, AZ means feeding a small waterproof camera into the home's sewer line to see its condition. The plumber watches a live view of the pipe walls, joints, and any buildup or damage. It is a planned visit, not an emergency repair, though you can schedule it through the same 24/7 line.

People usually book an inspection for one of three reasons. They are buying or selling a home, a line keeps clogging after cleaning, or they want to know the pipe's condition before spending on a repair. This page explains what a camera can show, what it cannot, and how to get a useful result.

If sewage is backing up right now, that is a cleaning job first. Read about sewer line cleaning and call the line.

What to do and what not to do before the camera visit

A little preparation makes the visit shorter and keeps everyone safe.

  • Stop running water and flushing if drains are slow or backing up.
  • Do not pour chemical drain cleaners into the line, because they can splash on the plumber and the camera.
  • Find the cleanout cap if you know where it is, and clear space around it.
  • Keep children and pets away from any area with sewage on the floor.
  • Write down when the problem started and which drains are affected.

When a camera look is worth the money

A camera is not needed for every drain problem. These are the situations where it usually pays off.

Buying a home

A seller's drains may run today and still hide a crack or root intrusion. A camera look before closing gives you facts to negotiate with.

Repeat main line clogs

If a line has blocked more than once, a camera can show why instead of clearing it again blindly.

Before a big repair

Seeing the pipe first helps decide between cleaning, spot repair, and replacement.

After a backup

Once the line is open, a look inside can show whether the cause is still there.

Planning remodel or landscaping

Knowing the pipe's condition helps before heavy digging or a new addition.

What a sewer camera can show

The camera shows what the lens can see as it moves through the pipe. That usually includes cracks, offset joints, root intrusion, heavy buildup, a bellied or sagging section where water pools, and objects that are stuck. A plumber can also note the pipe material when it is visible.

These findings are useful because each one leads to a different decision. Roots at a joint, a long crack, and a mild sag are not the same problem, and the fixes differ.

What a camera cannot tell you

A camera result is a snapshot of one pass through one pipe. These lists keep expectations realistic.

A camera can usually show

  • Visible cracks, breaks, and offset joints.
  • Roots entering the pipe.
  • Buildup, grease, and debris.
  • Sags where water collects.
  • Where the pipe changes direction or size.

A camera cannot reliably show

  • A leak on the outside of the pipe that is not visible inside.
  • The exact depth or location above ground without a separate locating tool.
  • How long the pipe will last.
  • Pipe beyond a blockage the camera cannot pass.
  • Conditions in water-filled sections where the lens cannot see.

How a planned inspection goes

Because it is planned, you can prepare. The visit usually takes this shape.

  1. Share the history

    Tell the plumber about past clogs, repairs, and any home inspection notes. This guides where to look.

  2. Find the entry point

    The camera usually goes in through a cleanout. Without one, the plumber may need to use a fixture opening.

  3. Pass the camera

    The camera is fed down the line while the plumber watches the screen and notes anything unusual.

  4. Mark what matters

    Many plumbers can note the distance to a problem and, with a locating tool, mark it above ground.

  5. Review the footage

    You see what the plumber saw, and ask for a recording if you want one.

  6. Talk about options

    If something needs work, you hear the choices and get a clear price before work starts.

Before the plumber arrives

A little preparation makes the inspection more useful.

  • Find the sewer cleanout, usually a capped pipe near the house, and make sure it can be reached.
  • Gather any past plumbing invoices or inspection reports for the home.
  • Write down which fixtures have been slow and how often.
  • Avoid running large amounts of water just before the visit unless the plumber asks.
  • If you are buying, ask the seller or agent about access.
  • Decide whether you want a recording of the footage.

Locating the pipe and Arizona 811

Some inspections include locating where the pipe runs. A plumber can use a locating tool with the camera to mark a spot above ground. That tells you where to dig before any repair.

Arizona law requires you to contact Arizona 811 before digging. The service is free, and utilities have two full working days to respond. Ask the plumber whether a locate will mark your private sewer line, and do not assume it will.

Pipe lining and what an inspection says about it

Some homeowners ask about lining a pipe instead of digging it up. Whether lining suits a pipe depends on its condition, and the camera is what shows that. Ask the plumber what they see and why lining or replacement fits. Our sewer line repair page covers how that choice is usually made.
