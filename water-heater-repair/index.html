Water Heater Repair in El Mirage, AZ

Water heater repair in El Mirage, AZ means finding out why a tank has stopped heating or started leaking, then fixing the part that failed or telling you honestly that the tank is finished. The 24/7 emergency line is open if water is on the floor right now. Shut the cold supply valve above the tank and keep reading while you wait.

Most calls fall into two groups. Either the water is lukewarm or cold, which is usually a heating part, or there is a puddle, which is sometimes a cheap fitting and sometimes the tank shell itself. The two look alike from the doorway and need very different answers.

This page walks through what each symptom usually means, how a gas unit differs from an electric one, and when a repair makes sense. If the tank is old and leaking from the body, replacing the heater is often the better use of your money.

When a leaking or dead water heater is an emergency

Water spreading across a floor, a tank that is hissing or rumbling hard, or any smell of gas near the heater should be treated as urgent at any hour. A tank holds a lot of water, and a failed one can empty onto flooring in a short time.

If you smell gas, do not touch switches or look for the source. Leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first, then call a plumber from outside.

  • Close the cold water shutoff on the pipe above the tank. The handle usually turns a quarter turn or several full turns.
  • For an electric heater, switch off its breaker. For a gas heater with no gas smell, turn the gas control to pilot or off.
  • Move boxes and stored items away from the water, and keep people off wet floors near outlets.
  • Call the 24/7 line and describe where the water is coming from, if you can see it.

A puddle at the tank: leak at a fitting or the tank itself

Where the water leaves matters more than how much there is. A weeping connection on the cold inlet or hot outlet, a drain valve that drips, or a relief valve that has opened can all be fixed without replacing the tank. These repairs are usually short and the heater keeps its remaining life.

A leak from the body of the tank is different. Steel tanks are lined with glass, and once the lining and steel give way, the shell seeps from inside. No patch or sealant holds a pressurized tank, and a plumber who says otherwise is selling you a short delay.

Water can also run along a pipe and drip somewhere else, which makes a fitting leak look like a tank leak. The plumber will dry the tank and fittings and watch where the first bead forms. If the shell is the source, the honest advice is to shut it down and plan the swap. See planned water heater installation if you have time to choose a unit instead of rushing.

A slow leak can also reach a slab or wall before you see it. If you find damp flooring away from the tank, leak detection can tell you whether the heater is the source or only the nearest wet spot.

No hot water: gas versus electric

The fuel type decides which parts can fail, so the first question on a no-hot-water call is gas or electric. Neither type is more reliable in general. They simply fail in different places.

Gas tank heaters

  • Common faults are a pilot that will not stay lit, a dirty burner, a worn thermocouple, or a failed gas control valve.
  • The plumber checks that gas is reaching the unit and that the flue is drawing before any repair.
  • Any gas smell means leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before anyone calls a plumber.
  • A new gas control valve can approach the cost of a discount tank, so age matters in the repair decision.

Electric tank heaters

  • Common faults are a tripped breaker, a failed upper or lower element, or a bad thermostat or high limit switch.
  • The plumber tests power and each part with a meter, which pins the fault down quickly.
  • Elements and thermostats are inexpensive parts, so repair usually makes sense on a tank that is otherwise sound.
  • Never open the access panel with the breaker on. Water and live wiring are a bad mix.

Hybrid heat pump units

Some homes have a hybrid electric heater that pulls heat from the surrounding air. These add a fan, a compressor, and condensate drainage to the usual tank parts. Ask the plumber whether the repair is within what the company services, since some hybrid faults go back to the manufacturer.

If a hybrid unit shows an error code, write it down before you call. The code narrows the fault much faster than a description of the symptoms.

What 7 grains per gallon does inside a tank

The city's 2024 water quality report lists groundwater hardness from 4 to 9 grains per gallon, with an average of 7. Check the latest report for current numbers.

Water at that hardness typically leaves scale on heating elements and on the tank floor over the years. Scale makes elements work harder and burners heat through a layer of sediment, which is a common reason tanks get noisy and run out of hot water sooner. A yearly flush slows the buildup, and a softener or conditioning system reduces it at the source.

How the plumber finds the fault

The order matters because it keeps the plumber from replacing parts that were never the problem.

  1. Confirm the supply

    The plumber checks that the cold shutoff is open and that gas or power is reaching the unit.

  2. Look for the first drop

    Fittings, the drain valve, and the relief valve line are dried and watched to find where water starts.

  3. Test the heating parts

    On electric units, a meter tests elements and thermostats. On gas units, the plumber checks ignition, the flame sensor, and the flue draft.

  4. Check pressure and age

    High household pressure can stress a tank and trip the relief valve. The data plate shows the age of the tank.

  5. Give you the options

    You get the repair choice and the replace choice, with the price of each, before work begins.

Repair or replace: where the line falls

Repair is worth it when the tank shell is sound and the failed part is cheap. These are the situations that usually decide it.

Repair makes sense

A failed element, thermostat, igniter, or fitting on a tank that has not leaked from the body is usually worth fixing.

Repair is a gamble

A tank near the end of its usual service life with a second failure in a short span often points to more trouble ahead.

Replace instead

Any leak from the tank body, heavy rust at the base, or a tank that fails repeatedly should be replaced rather than repaired.

Ask about the unit's age

The data plate lists the manufacture date. Ask the plumber to read it with you so the decision rests on facts.

Before the plumber gets there

A few minutes of preparation saves time on arrival and limits damage.

  • Photograph the data plate on the tank, including the model and serial number.
  • Note whether the unit is gas or electric and where the water or noise is coming from.
  • Clear a path to the heater and remove anything stored around it.
  • Do not drain a hot tank. The water can scald and the drain valve can fail.
  • Never plug or cap the pipe from the relief valve. It is a safety device.
