Kitchen Sink Faucet Repair Guide for El Mirage, AZ Homes

A symptom-first guide for El Mirage homeowners, with the safe limits of DIY spelled out.

The short answer

Most kitchen faucet problems come from four small parts: the cartridge or valve inside the handle, the O-rings at the spout base, the aerator on the tip, and the supply lines under the sink. A drip from the spout usually means a worn cartridge. Water at the base of the spout usually means worn O-rings. Weak flow usually means a clogged aerator.

You can handle an aerator cleaning and a cartridge swap with basic tools if the shutoff valves under the sink work. Call a plumber when the valves will not close, the faucet body is corroded or cracked, or water is getting into the cabinet and you cannot find where.

Check these first, before you take anything apart

Five minutes of looking saves a lot of unnecessary work.

  • Dry the faucet and the cabinet floor, then watch for 10 minutes to see exactly where the water appears.
  • Unscrew the aerator at the tip and look for white or tan grit. Scale in that grit is mineral buildup from the water.
  • Open and close both shutoff valves under the sink. If either one is stuck, leave it alone and plan to call a plumber.
  • Look at the faucet brand on the body or in the cabinet. Cartridge parts are brand and model specific.
  • Take a photo of the faucet and the supply lines before you start so you can match parts at the store.

How do you replace a faucet cartridge?

This is the most common repair for a drip. The details change by brand, so treat this as the general order of work.

  1. Shut off the water

    Close both shutoff valves under the sink. Open the faucet to drain pressure, and plug the drain so small screws cannot fall in.

  2. Remove the handle

    Look for a decorative cap, then a set screw or a screw under it. Lift the handle straight off.

  3. Pull the cartridge

    Remove the retaining nut or clip, then pull the cartridge straight up. Note which way the tabs face before it comes out.

  4. Match the part

    Bring the old cartridge to the store or match the model number. A near match often leaks or limits the hot and cold range.

  5. Clean and reassemble

    Wipe mineral scale from the valve body, seat the new cartridge, and reassemble in reverse order.

  6. Test slowly

    Open the shutoff valves slowly, then run the faucet and check the cabinet for drips for several minutes.

Repair or replace the whole faucet?

A repair kit is cheap in parts but not always the better choice. These lists show which way each situation tends to lean.

Repair usually makes sense when

  • One part has failed and the rest of the faucet looks clean.
  • The faucet is a recognizable brand with parts still sold.
  • The finish is intact and the handle moves smoothly.
  • You like the faucet and the sink holes match it.

Replacement usually makes sense when

  • You have repaired the same faucet more than once.
  • The body is pitted, cracked or crusted with scale.
  • Parts are no longer available for the model.
  • You want a pull-down sprayer or a different style anyway.

Why does the faucet drip again a few months later?

A repeat drip usually has one of three explanations. The new part was a near match instead of an exact one, the valve seat inside the faucet is worn and chews up each new seal, or scale is lifting the seal off its seat. A seat that is pitted cannot be fixed by swapping a cartridge.

Over-tightening causes it too. Cranking the retaining nut flattens seals and cracks plastic housings. Snug is enough, and a plumber can tell you quickly whether the body is worth another repair.

Where DIY stops

Stop and call if a shutoff valve will not close, because forcing it can snap the stem and leave you with no way to stop the water. Stop if you see green corrosion on supply lines or fittings, or if the leak is coming from inside the wall.

A plumber can replace shutoff valves, supply lines and the faucet itself. For a quick look at the options, see how faucet repair works from the plumber's side.

What affects the cost of a faucet repair?

Price depends on the job, not the faucet alone. Call for a clear price before work starts and ask what it includes.

Parts availability

A common cartridge is simple to source. A discontinued model can push the job toward replacement.

Shutoff valve condition

If the valves under the sink do not close, they usually get replaced first so the repair can happen safely.

Faucet type

A pull-down sprayer, touchless model or wall-mounted unit takes longer than a basic two-handle faucet.

Hidden damage

Water that reached the cabinet or the drain connection adds work beyond the faucet.

Fixture supplied or not

Replacing with a faucet you bought differs from the plumber supplying one. Ask who provides the part and what the warranty covers.

Questions to ask before a plumber starts

  • Is the price for the repair only, or does it include new shutoff valves and supply lines if they are needed?
  • If the faucet is not worth repairing, what are my replacement options and what changes in the price?
  • Are you licensed and insured for plumbing work in Arizona?
  • Will you test for leaks under the sink after the repair?
  • What happens to the old parts, and can I see the failed one?
