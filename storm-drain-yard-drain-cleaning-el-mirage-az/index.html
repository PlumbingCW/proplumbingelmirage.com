Cleaning Storm Drains and Yard Drains in El Mirage, AZ

Monsoon season ended September 30, which makes October through early June the quiet window to prepare.

The short answer

The storm drains in the street belong to the city, not to you, so do not try to open or clean them. What you can safely clear is on your own property: the grate of a yard drain or catch basin, the end of a downspout, a gutter and any shallow swale where leaves and dust collect. Pull debris by hand with gloves, rinse with a hose, and check that water leaves the area.

Anything underground, anything that smells like sewage, or any pipe you cannot see the end of is a job for a licensed plumber. The monsoon runs June 15 to September 30, so the best time to do this is before the middle of June.

Why bother in a desert that gets so little rain?

NOAA normals for the Youngtown station list about 8.34 inches of rain in an average year, and the National Weather Service says the monsoon can bring 30 to 60 percent of the annual total. That means a lot of water in a few events, and drains sit unused for months beforehand. Dust, leaves and trash settle in them while nobody is watching.

The same weather service lists flash floods, lightning, microbursts and dust storms among monsoon hazards. A storm that drops its rain in minutes will find any blocked drain on your lot.

A simple off-season calendar

Do these in the cool months, when working outside is comfortable.

  1. October to November

    Walk the property after any rain and note where water pools or runs toward the house. Fix small grading problems while they are easy to see.

  2. December to February

    Clear grates, gutters and downspout openings. Flush each yard drain with a hose and watch how fast it clears.

  3. March to April

    Look for roots, cracks and settling along buried pipe runs. Call a plumber if flushing does not clear a drain.

  4. May

    Do a second flush and test every downspout with a hose. Fix anything that backs up or leaks before the season starts.

  5. June 15

    Monsoon season begins. Keep a weekly walk-around habit through September 30.

What you can safely clear yourself

These tasks stay on your own property and use no special tools.

  • Wear gloves and closed shoes, and lift grates only if they are loose and meant to come off.
  • Remove leaves, trash, sediment and dust from the grate and the shallow basin below it.
  • Rinse with a garden hose and watch whether water drains away steadily.
  • Clear gutters and the openings at the top and bottom of each downspout.
  • Check that the end of each downspout directs water away from the foundation.
  • Take photos before and after so you can show a plumber what you found.

Safe limits: what to leave alone

Never reach into a storm drain, climb into one or use a pressure washer to push debris down a city inlet. Storm drains can hold sharp objects and, after rain, fast water. Do not pour chemicals, paint or yard waste into any drain either, because the water from a storm drain often leaves the area untreated.

If you need to dig to find a buried pipe, contact Arizona 811 first. It is free by law, utilities have two full working days to respond, and the marks last 15 working days. Hand tools only within 24 inches of the marks.

When a yard drain is slow after you cleared it

If the grate is clean but water still pools, the buried pipe is probably restricted by silt, roots or a sag. A garden hose cannot reach that, and a household drain snake may not fit. A plumber can clear the line with a cable or high-pressure water, and may use a camera to see where it narrows.

Ask the plumber to confirm where the line goes before cleaning it. A yard drain and a sewer cleanout can look alike, and it matters which one you are working on. For standard cable work, see drain cleaning, and for heavy silt or roots, hydro jetting uses pressurized water.

El Mirage monsoon facts that affect your drains

NWS Phoenix says monsoon season runs June 15 to September 30. Dust storms are on the National Weather Service's list of monsoon hazards, so expect fine sediment to wash into any yard drain that has been sitting open. Plan a flush in May and another soon after the season ends.

The city's website lists a Stormwater page among its water resources, and the city's customer service office is the starting point for questions about street inlets. We could not confirm a city phone line or reporting form for blocked inlets, so ask customer service how to report one.
