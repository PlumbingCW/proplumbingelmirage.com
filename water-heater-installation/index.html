Water Heater Installation in El Mirage, AZ

Water heater installation in El Mirage, AZ is the planned job of setting a new heater in a home or building, connecting it to water, gas or power, and venting it safely. The best time to do it is before an old tank fails, when you can choose the size and type without a flooded floor forcing the decision.

Most of the work and nearly all of the risk sits in the connections. Gas piping, flue venting, relief valve drainage, and shutoff valves matter more than the brand on the tank label.

If your heater has already failed and water is spreading, go to water heater replacement for the emergency steps instead. This page covers the unhurried version.

Gas smell? Leave first, then call

If you smell gas at any point before, during, or after an installation, do not search for the source and do not touch light switches or appliances. Leave the building right away and call the gas utility or 911 from outside.

Call a plumber only after the gas utility or emergency services say it is safe. No installer can promise a gas response time, and none should ask you to stay inside while they look.

From first call to hot water

Every home is different, but a planned install generally follows this path.

  1. Look at the space

    The plumber sees where the heater sits, what fuel it uses, and what the vent and drain look like now.

  2. Pick the type and size

    You choose between tank, tankless, or hybrid based on household size, fuel, and space.

  3. Confirm permit needs

    Ask the El Mirage building department whether the job needs a permit. Ask the plumber who handles that step.

  4. Do the install

    The plumber sets the unit, connects water, gas or power, vent, and relief valve drain, then fills and tests it.

  5. Walk through the controls

    You learn where the shutoffs are, how to set the temperature, and what a normal sound or smell is.

Gas line and venting: the safety work

A gas water heater needs a gas supply, a shutoff within reach, and a flue that carries exhaust outdoors. If the vent is blocked, undersized, or poorly pitched, fumes can enter the home. That is why a vent is checked on every gas install, new or replacement.

The city adopted the 2018 International Fuel Gas Code, and a local amendment requires underground fuel gas piping to be buried at least 12 inches deep, or 18 inches for plastic piping. This matters if your project adds a new underground gas run, such as for a detached garage. Ask the El Mirage building department what applies to your job.

Before anyone digs for a new line, Arizona law requires a call to Arizona 811. It is free, and utilities have two full working days to mark their lines. Hand tools only within 24 inches of marks is the usual rule, so ask your plumber how they handle it.

Choosing between tank and tankless

Both types heat water, but they suit different homes. Neither is the right answer for everyone.

Storage tank

  • Lower upfront cost and simple parts.
  • Holds a reserve, so it handles many taps at once until the tank runs low.
  • Needs floor space and loses some heat while it stands.
  • Scale builds on the tank floor in hard water, so it benefits from flushing.

Tankless

  • Heats on demand and saves space.
  • Often needs a larger gas line and a different vent than a tank.
  • Needs regular descaling, especially with hardness near 7 grains per gallon.
  • See the tankless water heater page for details.

Expansion tank and other add-ons: ask the plumber

When a water heater is fed through a check valve or a pressure regulator, heated water has nowhere to expand. The pressure can rise and make the relief valve drip. A thermal expansion tank is the usual fix.

Whether your home needs one depends on how your plumbing is set up, so ask the plumber. The same goes for drain pans, a sediment trap on the gas line, and mixing valves. Your installer should explain which items apply and why.

New construction and remodel installs

In a new build or a remodel, the heater location and its vent and drain are decided early. Getting these right before walls close avoids awkward pipe runs later. Share the fuel choice and the planned location with your plumber as soon as you have them.

For bigger scopes that also add fixtures and supply lines, see plumbing installation. It covers the wider piping work around the heater.

Install-day preparation list

  • Decide on fuel, tank size, and whether you want tank or tankless.
  • Clear the area around the old heater and the path to it.
  • Locate the main water shutoff and show it to the plumber.
  • Ask the plumber who will contact the building department about permits.
  • Plan for a few hours without hot water on install day.
