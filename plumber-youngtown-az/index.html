A 1954 retirement town and its small mid-century homes

Youngtown began in 1954, when 320 acres of farmland were turned into the first planned retirement community in the United States. The first 125 houses went up in 1955. Those early houses were about 750 square feet, which is small by today's standards. In a small older house, access under sinks and behind walls can be tight, so tell the dispatcher if a valve is hard to reach.

The town was incorporated in 1960. Homes of different ages can sit close together on the same block. You cannot guess a pipe type from a street name, so ask the plumber to look at what is actually in your wall, under your sink and at your water heater.

Today's zoning for most of the town is medium density residential, with lots from 6,000 square feet to more than 12,000. That is the current zoning, not a record of the original lots. For you, it means a mix of lot sizes and yard layouts, which matters when a sewer or water line has to be traced across a yard.

What older plumbing usually means for your home

We do not know what pipe material was used in any Youngtown home, and no public source we found says. What is generally true is that homes from the 1950s and 1960s typically still have some original parts, such as shutoff valves, supply lines and drain sections. Older parts often fail without warning, so ask the plumber to inspect them even when the repair you called about is small.

A plumber cannot judge a pipe by looking at the wall. The usual steps are checking visible lines, testing pressure and shutoffs, and running a camera or a leak test when a leak is suspected. Each method has limits, and a good plumber will tell you what a test can and cannot show before you pay for a bigger repair.

EPCOR hard water: 7 to 11 grains per gallon and no softening

EPCOR's 2025 water quality report for Sun City and Youngtown lists hardness from 7 to 11 grains per gallon. The report's own scale calls 7 to 10.4 hard and anything above that very hard, and EPCOR says it does not treat the water for hardness.

For you, that means mineral scale builds up in water heaters, faucet aerators and shower heads. Check your own latest EPCOR report, and ask a plumber about a softener or filter if scale keeps coming back.

Permits go through Youngtown Community Development

The Town of Youngtown Community Development Department handles inspections, including plumbing, and has adopted the 2021 International Plumbing Code and the 2021 International Residential Code. Town Hall is at 12030 N Clubhouse Square, and the office is open Monday to Friday, 8:00 to 4:00. Call the town to learn whether your job needs a permit, because we cannot say for you.

Contractors working in town need an Arizona Registrar of Contractors license and a Town of Youngtown business license. You can check the first one on the ROC website at roc.az.gov. Asking for both before work starts protects you if an inspection is required later.

Who to call first when something goes wrong

The order matters, and the first call is not always to a plumber.

  1. Smell gas? Leave and call 911 or the gas utility

    Do not use a phone, switch or flame inside. Call a plumber only after you are outside and the utility says it is safe.

  2. Water at the street or meter? Call EPCOR

    If the leak is on the utility side of the meter, it is a question for EPCOR. A plumber can help you tell which side it is on.

  3. Water inside your home? Shut the main and call the 24/7 line

    A plumber can walk you through the shutoff by phone and then dispatch.

  4. Ask about a permit before a big repair

    Call Community Development before replacing a water heater or running new pipe.

What to do before the plumber gets to your Youngtown door

A few minutes of prep can limit the damage while the 24/7 line sends someone out.

Find and close the main

The main shutoff is usually near the front of the house or at the meter. If the valve is stiff or will not close, tell the dispatcher.

Cut power near water

Turn off breakers for wet rooms, but do not touch a panel while standing in water. If you cannot do it safely, leave it and say so on the phone.

Clear the floor and save what matters

Lift rugs, move boxes and pull electronics, photos and papers to a dry spot.

Call and describe what you see

Say where the water is coming from, when the home was built and whether the water is clear or dark. That lets the plumber plan the right parts.
